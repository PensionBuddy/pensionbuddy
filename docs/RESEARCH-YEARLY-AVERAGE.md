## 2. The denominator: CONFIRMED

**Start:** the later of (a) 5 January 1953 (man) / 6 July 1953 (woman), or
(b) **the beginning of the contribution year in which entry into insurance
occurred**. It is the start of the contribution year, not the date of the
first contribution: a person who first paid PRSI in August 1978 counts the
whole 1978/79 year.

**End:** **the end of the last complete contribution year before attaining
pensionable age, or deferred pensionable age, as the case may be.** For
someone taking the pension at 66, that is the tax year before their 66th
birthday.

**Source: Social Welfare Consolidation Act 2005, s.108(2), definition of
"yearly average"**
https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2005/act/26/section/108/enacted/en/html

> "'yearly average' means, subject to subsection (3), in relation to any
> claimant, the average per contribution year of contribution weeks in respect
> of which that claimant has qualifying contributions, voluntary contributions
> or credited contributions in the period beginning— (a) on 5 January 1953 in
> case the claimant is a man, or on 6 July 1953 in case the claimant is a
> woman, or (b) at the beginning of the contribution year in which the
> claimant's entry into insurance occurred (where after 5 January 1953 in the
> case of a man, or 6 July 1953 in the case of a woman), (whichever is the
> later) and ending at the end of the last complete contribution year before
> the date of his or her attaining pensionable age;"

The words "or deferred pensionable age, as the case may be" were inserted by
**s.44(c)(iv) of the Social Welfare (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2023
(No. 37 of 2023)**:
https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2023/act/37/enacted/en/print

**Counting method (DSP operational):** gov.ie, "How to calculate your State
Pension (Contributory) rate":
https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/publications/how-to-calculate-your-state-pension-contributory-rate/

> "Count all the number of years from the year of entry into insurable
> employment to the last completed tax year before your chosen pension
> drawdown date. Include the last completed tax year in the count."

Worked example on the same page: entry August 1978, drawdown 2023, count
1978 to 2022 inclusive: *"2022-1978 = 44 +1"* = **45 years**. The divisor is
inclusive of both endpoint years.

**Rounding: to the nearest whole number, half rounds up. CONFIRMED as DSP
practice.** gov.ie, "Operational Guidelines: State Pension Contributory"
(last updated 30 April 2026), under "Points to note, Rounding up":
https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/publications/operational-guidelines-state-pension-contributory/

> "From July 1992 in calculating the yearly average, a fraction of a whole
> number consisting of one half or more is rounded up to the nearest whole
> number e.g. 9.5 is rounded up to 10, 47.5 is rounded up to 48."

Citizens Information agrees: *"Your yearly average is rounded to the nearest
number. For example, 9.4 is rounded down to 9 and 47.5 is rounded up to 48."*

**The 2001 short tax year.** gov.ie, same how-to-calculate page:

> "There is a short tax year in the 2001 year, due to the change in the tax
> years (which creates an additional year in calculating the yearly average)."

Read with the worked example (1978 to 2022 = 45, which spans 2001), the
labelled years 1978, 1979 … 2000, 2001, 2002 … 2022 already form a contiguous
sequence in which the short 2001 year is one entry. So "end year minus entry
year", with the end year being the year before drawdown, counts it.

## 3. The numerator, and HomeCaring Periods: CONFIRMED

**Paid + credited + voluntary.** Credits are included, and, unlike TCA,
**there is no cap on credits** under Yearly Average.

**HomeCaring Periods do NOT count under the Yearly Average method. They are
TCA-only.**

**Source 1: gov.ie, "How to calculate your State Pension (Contributory)
rate", section "HomeCaring Periods":**

> "**The HomeCaring Periods Scheme is only used in a Total Contributions
> Approach calculation.**"

And the mirror statement on the same page, section "Homemaker's Scheme":

> "**The Homemaker's Scheme is only used in a Yearly Average Method
> calculation.**"

**Source 2: the statutory definition (s.108(2), SWCA 2005)** lists only
three categories and does not include home caring periods:

> "…the average per contribution year of contribution weeks in respect of
> which that claimant has **qualifying contributions, voluntary contributions
> or credited contributions**…"

**Source 3: gov.ie, "State Pension (Contributory) calculation methods and
rates", section "Yearly Average Method":**

> "Having satisfied the minimum qualification criteria, when deciding on the
> rate payable using the Yearly Average calculation method, this calculation
> method considers **two elements**: full-rate social Insurance (PRSI)
> Contributions [and] credited contributions (credits)… (**there is no cap on
> the number of credits which can be used in the calculation**)"

That same page lists **three** elements for TCA, the third being HomeCaring
periods.

**What caring time does under Yearly Average:** the **Homemaker's Scheme**
removes whole years from the *denominator* rather than adding to the
numerator. gov.ie, same page:

> "Under the Homemaker's Scheme, any years that you spent as a homemaker
> (since 6 April 1994) are ignored or disregarded when working out your
> yearly average contributions for a State Pension (Contributory). Only full
> years can be ignored or disregarded."

Statutory basis, SWCA 2005 s.108(3):

> "In the case of a claimant who was a homemaker for the duration of any
> complete contribution year, beginning on or after 6 April 1994, in which
> the claimant does not have any credited contributions or voluntary
> contributions, that contribution year shall be disregarded for the purposes
> of determining the yearly average of that claimant subject to the total
> number of contribution years so disregarded not exceeding 20."

Four constraints on that disregard: **only from 6 April 1994**, **only
complete contribution years**, **maximum 20 years**, and **only a year in
which the claimant has no credited or voluntary contributions**.

This is the most consequential asymmetry in the system: a person with long
caring gaps typically does far better under TCA (HomeCaring Periods, up to
1,040, added to the numerator) than under Yearly Average (Homemaker years
merely removed from the denominator, and only post-1994).

## 4. Transition proportions 2025 to 2034: CONFIRMED in primary legislation

| Year of drawdown | Yearly Average share | TCA share |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 90% | 10% |
| 2026 | 80% | 20% |
| 2027 | 70% | 30% |
| 2028 | 60% | 40% |
| 2029 | 50% | 50% |
| 2030 | 40% | 60% |
| 2031 | 30% | 70% |
| 2032 | 20% | 80% |
| 2033 | 10% | 90% |
| 2034 onwards | 0% | 100%, TCA only |

**Source: Social Welfare (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2023 (No. 37 of
2023), s.46(e), inserting s.109(6D) into the Social Welfare Consolidation
Act 2005**
https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2023/act/37/enacted/en/print

> "(6D) Notwithstanding any other provision in this Chapter, the rate of State
> pension (contributory) payable to a claimant who attains pensionable age or
> deferred pensionable age, as the case may be, on or after 1 January 2025
> shall be calculated as follows:
> (b) in the case of a claimant who attains pensionable age or deferred
> pensionable age, as the case may be, **between 1 January 2026 and 31
> December 2026**, either—
> (i) the age referenced rate of State pension (contributory) payable to the
> claimant calculated according to the aggregated contributions method, or
> (ii) the aggregate of—
> (I) the age referenced rate of State pension (contributory) payable to the
> claimant, calculated in accordance with the yearly average approach
> **multiplied by 80 per cent**, and
> (II) the age referenced rate of State pension (contributory) payable to the
> claimant, calculated in accordance with the aggregated contributions method
> **multiplied by 20 per cent**,
> whichever is the more favourable to that claimant;"

Paragraphs (a) through (i) run 2025 to 2033 on the same pattern; paragraph
(j) closes it out:

> "(j) in the case of a claimant who attains pensionable age or deferred
> pensionable age, as the case may be, on or after 1 January 2034, **solely in
> accordance with the aggregated contributions method**."

("Aggregated contributions method" is the statutory name for TCA.)

Corroborated administratively in SW19 2026, p.32 "Calculation Strategy"
table, and in gov.ie's Operational Guidelines and calculation-methods pages.

## 5. Which year governs: CONFIRMED

**The year in which the person attains pensionable age (66) OR, if they
defer, the year in which they attain their deferred pensionable age, that is,
the year of drawdown.** Not the year of claim, and not the current year.

For someone taking the pension at 66 these coincide. For a deferrer they do
not, and the drawdown year wins.

**Source 1: the statute.** s.109(6D) keys each paragraph to a claimant "who
attains pensionable age **or deferred pensionable age, as the case may be**"
within the stated calendar year. "Deferred pensionable age" is defined in
s.108(2) (inserted by s.44(c)(i) of the 2023 Act):

> "'deferred pensionable age' means, in relation to a person born on or after
> 1 January 1958 who has attained pensionable age— (a) **the age at which he
> or she is awarded a State pension (contributory)**, or (b) the age of 70
> years, whichever is the lesser age and, in any event, does not apply to a
> person born on or before 31 December 1957"

**Source 2: gov.ie, "How to calculate your State Pension (Contributory)
rate", section "Combined rate approach":**

> "The combined rate approach in each of the years 2025 – 2033 is set out
> below and is **determined by the year you draw down your State Pension
> (Contributory)**. Once your rate is calculated, that will be your relevant
> rate of State Pension (Contributory), subject to future budgetary changes.
> **Note: If you choose to drawdown your State pension (Contributory) between
> age 67 and 70, the year you drawdown your pension will determine the
> combined rate approach used, and not the year you reach pensionable age
> (66 years).**"

**Source 3: Operational Guidelines:** *"This is based on the date you draw
down your pension and not your date of birth/year you turn pension age"*

**Source 4: SW19 2026, p.35:** *"Note: If you choose to draw down your SPC
between the ages of 67 to 70, the year you draw down your pension will
determine the calculation strategy used, and not the year you reach
pensionable age (66 years)."*

> **Watch this.** Citizens Information phrases it loosely as *"If you reach
> pension age in 2026, only 80% of the yearly average … rate will apply"*.
> That wording is right only for people drawing down at 66 and is wrong for
> deferrers. Deferral is available to everyone born on or after 1 January
> 1958.

The blend percentage is locked at drawdown; it does not re-ratchet each year.
s.108(2): *"once an age referenced rate of State pension (contributory) has
been so awarded to a claimant, that rate shall remain payable to the claimant
concerned for the remaining period of his or her claim"*.

## 6. The 520 minimum: CONFIRMED, and it is on PAID contributions

**The same 520 gate applies to a Yearly Average award**, restated explicitly
as a precondition in the new machinery. It is defined in terms of **paid**
contributions. **Credits do not count toward the 520.**

**Source 1: S.I. No. 592/2024, Article 62D(2):**

> "(2) Sub-article (1) shall apply in the case of a claimant –
> (a) who has a yearly average of not less than 10, and
> (b) **who has qualifying contributions in respect of not less than 520
> contribution weeks since entry into insurance.**"

**Source 2: the statutory meaning of "qualifying contribution", SWCA 2005
s.2(1):**
https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2005/act/26/section/2/enacted/en/html

> "'qualifying contribution' means the appropriate employment contribution or
> self-employment contribution **which was paid** or would have been paid but
> for section 13(2)(c) in respect of any insured person or the appropriate
> optional contribution which was paid or would have been paid but for
> section 29(1)(b);"

**Source 3: extended to Long-Term Carer's contributions.** s.44(c)(i) of the
2023 Act inserted into s.108(2): *"'qualifying contribution' other than in
respect of a specified contribution week, includes a long-term carer's
qualifying contribution;"*

**Source 4: Operational Guidelines, "Second qualifying condition - number of
full-rate contributions paid":**

> "a person reaching drawdown date on or after 6 April 2012 must have **paid
> 520 full-rate employment contributions**, or, if at least 260 full-rate
> employment contributions are paid, **the balance of the 520 can be made up
> with high rate voluntary contributions**"

**Net rule:** the 520 is satisfied by paid full-rate employment or
self-employment contributions, plus Long-Term Carer's Contributions (once
1,040 are accrued), plus high-rate voluntary contributions above a floor of
260 paid. **Credits never count toward the 520.**

A terminology trap: gov.ie and Citizens Information describe this as "520
Reckonable Contributions (Column 5)". Column 5 of the Contribution Statement
is paid contributions plus LTCC; credits sit separately in Column 6. So
"reckonable" there means Column 5, not "paid + credited".

**This gate is a condition of entitlement to the pension itself, not of a
particular method.** It is identical whether the award comes out via TCA or
via the Yearly Average blend.

## 7. Interaction rules

All CONFIRMED unless marked.

**(a) The best-of applies to everyone attaining pensionable age or deferred
pensionable age on or after 1 January 2025.** No cohort carve-out. Those
awarded before 2025 stay on the old rules (S.I. 592/2024 Explanatory Note).

**(b) A yearly average below 10 removes Method 2 entirely.** Article
62D(2)(a) requires a yearly average of *"not less than 10"*. Below that there
is no Yearly Average rate to blend, and the person falls back to TCA only,
which can still pay since TCA has no yearly-average floor, only the 520 gate.
A calculator must not blend a €0 Yearly Average rate; it drops to Method 1.

**(c) "Yearly average approach" statutorily means the better of Yearly
Average and Alternative Yearly Average.** s.108(2): *"'yearly average
approach' means the rate of State pension (contributory) to which a claimant
would be entitled to on the basis of the claimant's yearly average or
alternative yearly average whichever is more favourable to him or her"*. The
Alternative Yearly Average runs from 6 April 1979 and, per gov.ie, applies
only if it produces at least 48 (*"If it is less than 48, the Yearly Average
Method calculation will be used."*). A Class S variant using 1988 as entry
applies only if it produces at least 10.

**(d) Maximum 52 contributions counted per year.** gov.ie: *"For each year, a
maximum of 52 contributions are considered for State Pension (Contributory)
purposes."*

**(e) TCA caps that do not apply under Yearly Average.** TCA: credits capped
at 520; HomeCaring Periods capped at 1,040; **combined credits + HomeCaring
capped at 1,040**. Operational Guidelines: *"When both credits and HCP are on
a record, 1040 is the maximum number of combined HCP and credits than can be
used in the calculation."* Under Yearly Average, credits are uncapped and
HomeCaring Periods are unusable.

**(f) TCA percentage is rounded to 2 decimal places.** gov.ie: *"% of maximum
rate which is rounded to 2 decimal places"*. The yearly average is rounded
to a whole number; the TCA percentage to two decimals.

**(g) Long-Term Carer's Contributions are all-or-nothing at 1,040 and count
as full-rate paid.** They also disapply the entry-before-56 condition.

**(h) Deferral rates are age-referenced.** SW19 2026 p.35, TCA-100% maxima:
age 66 €299.30, 67 €313.40, 68 €328.90, 69 €345.70, 70 €363.90, with the
uplift *"reduced proportionally"* below 100% TCA.

**(i) Increase for a Qualified Adult under the new rules is proportionate,
not banded.** S.I. 592/2024 Article 62E and SW19 2026 p.34.

**(j) Entry into insurance is not always the first PRSI contribution.**
Mixed-rate contributors whose first full-rate contribution was before 6 April
1991 may use that date if more advantageous; a person who started Class S on
6 April 1988 having previously paid employee PRSI may use either date,
*"whichever would give you a higher rate of pension."*

**(k) Time on a "specified weekly payment" after 66 reduces the deferred
pensionable age.** SWCA 2005 s.109(1B).

**(l) Pro-rata pensions (EU/bilateral, mixed insurance, pre-1953, special
partial) use entirely different formulas.** A general calculator should
exclude these cases explicitly.

## UNCONFIRMED. Read before publishing.

1. **Exact commencement date in January 2026 for the €299.30 rate.** Budget
   2026 says "from January 2026". Safe wording: "from January 2026". Do not
   publish a specific day.

2. **The legal instrument that enacts the yearly-average rounding rule.** The
   rule is confirmed from two primary DSP and Citizens Information sources
   and is safe to publish. Its statutory source is not. Publish the rule; do
   not cite a section number for it.

3. **Whether the blended Method 2 result is rounded, and to what.** No
   rounding rule was found for the combined (YA × n%) + (TCA × m%) figure.
   SWCA 2005 s.109(20) prescribes rounding to the nearest 10 cent but is
   confined to the pre-1953 half-rate pensions and does not govern the 6D
   blend. **If a calculator displays cents, the final rounding behaviour is
   unverified.**

4. **Yearly Average band rates for deferred ages 67 to 70 in 2026.** SW19
   2026 publishes the bands only for age 66. Schedule 8C has the full grid
   but at 2024 amounts. **Do not interpolate from the age-66 column or scale
   by the TCA uplift ratio.**

5. **Whether "one half or more rounds up" survives unchanged into the 6D
   regime.** Very likely, from the general section of the Operational
   Guidelines updated 30 April 2026. Low risk.

6. **gov.ie's own rate pages are stale.** Not an unconfirmed figure, but a
   live inconsistency on the Department's site that a reader may raise. Cite
   SW19 2026 (p.33) for the band amounts.
