How we worked this out
  • Not included: your old pensions, product charges, your employer’s own scheme. Eligibility and opting out depend on your age, earnings and any workplace scheme; this tool does not check them.
  • Auto-enrolment is worked out on salary up to €80,000, for you, your employer and the State alike. It comes out of take-home pay, with no tax relief. The rates rise in three-year steps, always 3 from you, 3 from your employer, 1 from the State:
    1. This year2026 to 20281.5%, 1.5%, 0.5%
    2. This year2029 to 20313%, 3%, 1%
    3. This year2032 to 20344.5%, 4.5%, 1.5%
    4. This year2035 onward6%, 6%, 2%

    YouYour employerThe State

  • Rates from 2029 come from summaries, not the law itself. Check gov.ie before relying on them. Payroll stops the deduction after the pay period in which salary passes €80,000, so a little can be paid above it; a yearly figure ignores that.
  • A personal pension or Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA) gets tax relief at 40% on the part of your salary above your standard rate cut-off point, and 20% below it. Revenue’s 2026 cut-off points: single, €44,000; married or civil partners with one income, €53,000; with two incomes, up to €88,000, where the lower earner has at least €35,000 of their own. Relief is limited to an age-related share of earnings: 15% under 30, rising in steps to 40% at 60 and over, on earnings up to €115,000. Above that limit a payment still goes in, without relief. Pay-Related Social Insurance (PRSI) and the Universal Social Charge (USC) are not modelled.
  • “Instead of”: the personal pension is whatever costs you the same as auto-enrolment that year. A personal pension has no automatic employer or State payment. It is not a suggestion to leave or reduce auto-enrolment.
  • “On top of”: My Future Fund takes nothing above its set rate, as of September 2026, so the extra is a separate personal pension payment. If that changes, so does this.
  • By 66: every year from your age to 66, at that year’s rates and your age that year, grown at 5% a year (risk level 4 of 7, about half shares and half bonds). Real returns differ and can be negative.
  • A personal pension or PRSA has wider fund choice than My Future Fund, from the provider’s set menu.
