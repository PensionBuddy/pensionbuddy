Choosing your funds, and how much risk

My Future Fund invests your money in a small set of standard funds, with limited choice. A personal pension arranged through a broker gives access to a wider range of funds, and lets you choose a risk level that suits you. That applies to the personal pension here, whether it is instead of auto-enrolment or on top of it.

Funds carry a risk rating from 1 (lowest) to 7 (highest), the same scale you will see on any fund’s Key Information Document. Three illustrations, not a menu:

Higher potential returns mean a higher risk of loss. The value of a pension can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you put in. These figures are illustrations, not a recommendation of any level, and not a forecast. Which level suits you depends on things this page cannot see, and choosing one is a conversation with Damian.

Under auto-enrolment on its own, your money goes into My Future Fund’s standard funds and the choice is limited to those.

The assumptions behind these numbers

  • All figures are one year of contributions at your salary, not a retirement fund projection. They ignore investment growth, product charges and inflation.
  • Auto-enrolment rates are 1.5% employee, 1.5% employer and 0.5% State in years 1 to 3. They rise in three-year steps to 6%, 6% and 2% from year 10. The ratio is always 3 employee, 3 employer, 1 State. The phase slider starts on this year’s phase.

  • Payroll stops the deduction after the pay period in which salary passes €80,000. So a little can be paid above it. This yearly illustration ignores that.
  • My Future Fund takes nothing above its set rate, as of September 2026. If that changes, so does Mode 2.
  • A personal pension or Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA) gets tax relief at 40% above your standard rate cut-off point, and 20% below it. Relief is limited to Revenue’s age-related share of earnings: 15% under 30, up to 40% at 60 and over, on earnings up to €115,000. Above that limit a contribution still goes in, with no relief. Pay-Related Social Insurance (PRSI) and the Universal Social Charge (USC) are not modelled. In Mode 2 the top-up is a separate contribution.
  • Standard rate cut-off points are Revenue’s 2026 figures. Single: €44,000. Married or civil partners, one income: €53,000, whatever they earn. Two incomes: up to €88,000, only where the lower earner has at least €35,000 of their own. These change most years.
  • Money above the cap is your own rate on salary above €80,000. Auto-enrolment never takes it. The personal pension figure uses this page’s relief. It is not a suggestion to leave or reduce auto-enrolment.
  • A personal pension has no automatic employer or State contribution. Employer figures are zero unless you set them, and apply only if your employer agrees.
  • Eligibility and opting out depend on your age, earnings and any workplace scheme. This tool does not check them.
  • The three risk illustrations use the 1 to 7 Summary Risk Indicator that European Union (EU) rules require on every fund’s Key Information Document. The return figures are long-term annual assumptions for illustration, chosen to sit inside the 1% to 8% range the site’s other calculators use and with the medium level at their 5% default, so no two pages imply different growth. The “bad year” figures are rough indications of a fall, not a limit: a fund can lose more. All six figures are placeholders awaiting confirmation against the fund ranges Gresham can actually arrange.
  • A personal pension or PRSA has wider fund choice than My Future Fund, from the provider’s set menu. This page claims access to no particular asset class.
