How we worked this out
  • “You put in” is what leaves your own pocket each month. Under auto-enrolment it is a share of your pay, so it says what your pay is; we do not ask.
  • Auto-enrolment (My Future Fund) comes out of take-home pay, with no tax relief: the State adds €1 for every €3 you put in instead. You, your employer and the State pay a share of pay up to €80,000, so what you put in stops at that share of €80,000. The rates rise in three-year steps, always 3 from you, 3 from your employer, 1 from the State, and the limit rises with them:

  • Rates from 2029 come from summaries, not the law itself. Check gov.ie before relying on them. Payroll stops the deduction after the pay period in which pay passes €80,000, so a little can be paid above it; a monthly figure ignores that.
  • A personal pension or Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA) gets tax relief at your top rate of income tax: each €1 in costs you 80c at 20%, 60c at 40%. We assume all of it gets relief at the rate you pick. Relief is limited to an age-related share of earnings: 15% under 30, rising in steps to 40% at 60 and over, on earnings up to €115,000. Above that limit a payment still goes in, without relief. Pay-Related Social Insurance (PRSI) and the Universal Social Charge (USC) are not modelled. Your employer pays in only if they agree to.
  • Best of both: My Future Fund takes nothing above its set rate, as of September 2026, so anything more you put in goes to a personal pension. If that changes, so does this. Nothing here suggests leaving or reducing auto-enrolment.
  • Not included: growth, product charges, your old pensions, your employer’s own scheme. Who is auto-enrolled, and opting out, depend on your age, earnings and any workplace scheme; this tool does not check them.
  • A personal pension or PRSA has wider fund choice than My Future Fund, from the provider’s set menu.
