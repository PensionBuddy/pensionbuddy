Directors’ pensions in 2026: what changed.

Three things changed in the last two years. Four questions show which apply to you.

Rules as at 8 October 2026.

Executive pensions

An executive pension is a company pension for one person. It is also called a one-member arrangement. If it was set up before 22 April 2021, it had five years’ grace from the governance rules. They come from the European Union (EU) directive on institutions for occupational retirement provision (IORP II). That grace ended on 21 April 2026.

Since 22 April 2026, it can carry on only under the full rules. These are the rules for occupational schemes. They include at least two trustees, or a company trustee with two directors. Risk management and internal audit roles must be filled. Written policies are needed. So is an own-risk assessment. Investment rules apply. There can be no new borrowing. The Pensions Authority told trustees to find out what they must do to comply. Or their options to move.

Most have moved or are moving. They go to a master trust, a PRSA or a buy-out bond. A move to a PRSA is not allowed after the scheme’s normal retirement age. The Pensions Authority counted 40,644 one-member arrangements. That was on 1 September 2026. Some were being wound up. There were 141,500 in January 2023.

Sources: the Pensions Authority’s notice of 23 March 2026 and its conference figures of 15 September 2026; Statutory Instrument (S.I.) No. 128 of 2021; Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 13. A one-member scheme set up on or after 22 April 2021 has had to meet the full rules since 1 July 2022.

Company payments into a Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA)

Since 1 January 2025, you pay no tax on company payments into your PRSA, up to a limit. The limit is 100% of your pay from the company that year. The company can deduct them. Pay means your salary, bonuses and other benefits. Above 100%, you pay tax on the extra, as on your pay. Revenue calls it a benefit-in-kind. The company cannot deduct it.

A company scheme such as a master trust is not held to this 100% test. Revenue’s maximum funding rules limit it instead. The limits depend on salary, service and age.

Sources: Finance Act 2024, section 12; Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 24.

The October window

Do you pay into a PRSA or a personal pension yourself? Or do you pay into AVCs (additional voluntary contributions)? Those payments can count against last year’s income. This works if you pay by 31 October and choose to. For 2025 income that is 31 October 2026. Do you both pay and file through the Revenue Online Service (ROS)? Then it is 18 November 2026.

The company cannot backdate its payments. It deducts them in the accounting period it pays them. So its year end is the date that matters.

Sources: Revenue Pensions Manual, appendix III and chapters 4 and 24; Revenue eBrief 034/26.

The Standard Fund Threshold

The cap on pension savings with full tax relief is €2.2 million in 2026. It is €2.4 million from 1 January 2027. It rises by €200,000 a year to €2.8 million in 2029. Then it rises with earnings. Retirement lump sums taxed at 20% or less have a €500,000 limit. That limit does not change.

Small self-administered schemes

A small self-administered scheme still needs a pensioneer trustee. Revenue must approve that trustee. The scheme cannot lend to its members or the company. It cannot buy property from the company. It cannot let property to the company either. If it has one member, the end of the grace covers it too. That means no new borrowing.

Sources: Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 19; Pensions Act 1990, section 61B.

Four questions

The answer lists topics, not a recommendation. What suits you depends on much more than this page asks.

Do you have a company pension set up for you alone before 22 April 2021?
Would you like your company to pay in more than your pay from it in a year?
Is your scheme’s normal retirement age close, or already past?
Could all your pensions together come near €2.2 million (€2.4 million from 1 January 2027)?
