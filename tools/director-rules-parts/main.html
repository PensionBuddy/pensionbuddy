Directors’ pensions in 2026: what changed.

Three things changed in the last two years. Four questions show which apply to you.

Executive pensions

An executive pension, or one-member arrangement, is a company pension for one person. If set up before 22 April 2021, it had five years’ grace from the governance rules of the European Union (EU) directive on institutions for occupational retirement provision (IORP II). That grace ended on 21 April 2026.

Since 22 April 2026, it can carry on only under the full rules for occupational schemes. These include at least two trustees, or a company trustee with two directors. Risk management and internal audit roles must be filled. Written policies and an own-risk assessment are needed. Investment rules apply, with no new borrowing. The Pensions Authority told trustees to find out their compliance obligations or options for moving.

Most have moved or are moving: to a master trust, a Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA) or a buy-out bond. A move to a PRSA is not allowed after the scheme’s normal retirement age. The Pensions Authority counted 40,644 one-member arrangements on 1 September 2026, some being wound up. There were 141,500 in January 2023.

Sources: the Pensions Authority’s notice of 23 March 2026 and its conference figures of 15 September 2026; Statutory Instrument (S.I.) No. 128 of 2021; Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 13. A one-member scheme set up on or after 22 April 2021 has had to meet the full rules since 1 July 2022.

Company payments into a Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA)

Since 1 January 2025, company payments into your PRSA are tax-free for you, up to 100% of your pay from the company that year. The company can deduct them. Pay means salary, bonuses and other benefits. Above 100%, the excess is a taxable benefit-in-kind for you. The company cannot deduct it.

A company scheme such as a master trust is not held to this 100% test. Revenue’s maximum funding rules limit it instead, by salary, service and age.

Sources: Finance Act 2024, section 12; Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 24.

The October window

Your own payments into a PRSA, a personal pension or AVCs (additional voluntary contributions) can count against the previous year’s income. This works if you pay by 31 October and choose to. For 2025 income that is 31 October 2026. It is 18 November 2026 if you both pay and file through the Revenue Online Service (ROS).

Company payments cannot be backdated. The company deducts them in the accounting period it pays them. So its year end is the date that matters.

Sources: Revenue Pensions Manual, appendix III and chapters 4 and 24; Revenue eBrief 034/26.

The Standard Fund Threshold

The cap on pension savings with full tax relief is €2.2 million in 2026. It rises by €200,000 a year to €2.8 million in 2029, then with earnings. The €500,000 limit on retirement lump sums taxed at 20% or less does not change.

Small self-administered schemes

A small self-administered scheme still needs a Revenue-approved pensioneer trustee. It cannot lend to its members or the company. It cannot buy property from the company or let property to it. With one member, the end of the grace covers it too: no new borrowing.

Sources: Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 19; Pensions Act 1990, section 61B.
