What it is

Proposed · as at 25 September 2026

Official documents call it the Investment Account, and the media the Savings and Investment Account (SIA). All three are the same account.

Confirmed, and still to come

Confirmed in the Roadmap

Proposed · as at 25 September 2026

  • For Irish tax residents aged 18 or over with a Personal Public Service Number (PPSN). One account per person.
  • Holds listed shares, bonds, funds, ETFs and insurance-based investments. Not crypto assets or derivatives.
  • Cash can sit in it only briefly, and earns nothing.
  • No tax relief on what goes in.
  • A flat yearly tax on the average value above a tax-free threshold, due even in a falling year.
  • No deemed disposal, exit tax or Capital Gains Tax (CGT) inside it. The provider pays the tax.
  • No lock-in: withdraw at any time.
  • Not a deposit, and not capital-guaranteed. Values can fall.

Source for every item: Department of Finance, Roadmap for the Taxation of Retail Investment, 31 August 2026, published on gov.ie.

Still to come

Proposed · as at 25 September 2026

  • The tax-free threshold, tax rate and annual limit: to be announced on 6 October 2026, in Budget 2027.
  • When accounts open: during 2027.
  • The law itself: it is a proposal, and is not yet law.
  • Providers and their fees: not yet published.

Figures you may have read for these are not confirmed, and not used here.

Tax today, outside the account

  • Funds and ETFs: 38% exit tax on the gain, and on a deemed disposal every eight years. Source: Revenue, Tax and Duty Manual Part 27-01A-02, Investment Undertakings (updated January 2026).
  • Shares: 33% CGT on the gain, after an exemption of €1,270 a year. Source: Revenue, How to calculate CGT.
  • Deposits: 33% Deposit Interest Retention Tax (DIRT) on the interest. Source: Revenue, What DIRT rate is applicable?

All three checked on 25 September 2026.

Who it might suit

Proposed · as at 25 September 2026

Your employer pays into a pension

A pension gets employer money and tax relief. The PIA gets neither.

You run your own company

Your company can pay into your pension. A PIA is paid from your own money. See pensions for company directors.

You need the money before 60

A pension is normally locked until 60. The PIA is not.

Pension, PIA or both

It depends on your employer, your tax, and when you need the money.

Assumptions

  • Only the pension adds tax relief: at your rate, within Revenue’s age-related limit each year.
  • All three grow at the same rate, monthly, with payments at each month end.
  • PIA tax: your rate on the average of twelve month-end values above your threshold, taken each year end. How the average will be measured is not yet published. No annual limit is applied: none is announced.
  • The ETF accumulates. Each month’s purchase has a deemed disposal every eighth anniversary: 38% of the gain since the last, paid by selling units. At the end all is sold, with 38% exit tax on any gain left. A fall after a deemed disposal gets that tax back, up to the tax on the fall.
  • The pension is taken at the end, in one go: a quarter as a lump sum in Revenue’s bands, the rest as income at your rate. In practice the rest is usually drawn over years, perhaps at another rate.
