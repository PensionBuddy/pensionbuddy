Proposed, not yet law

The new Personal Investment Account (PIA), next to a pension.

A new account for investing, with its own simpler tax: 1% a year on the value above €50,000. Opens 1 July 2027.

Proposed · as at 8 October 2026, after Budget 2027 (6 October 2026). It becomes law only when the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 is passed.

What it is

Proposed · as at 8 October 2026

Budget 2027 calls it the Investment Account, and Revenue the Irish Investment Account. The media also call it the Savings and Investment Account (SIA). We call it the PIA. All are the same account.

Confirmed, and still to come

Announced

Proposed · as at 8 October 2026

  • For Irish tax residents aged 18 or over with a Personal Public Service Number (PPSN). One account per person.
  • Holds listed shares, bonds, funds, ETFs and insurance-based investments. Not crypto assets or derivatives.
  • Cash can sit in it only briefly, and earns nothing.
  • No tax relief on what goes in. Up to €12,000 a year can go in.
  • A flat 1% a year on the average daily value above €50,000. It is due even in a falling year.
  • Opens 1 July 2027.
  • No deemed disposal, exit tax, dividend withholding tax or Capital Gains Tax (CGT) inside it. The provider works out and pays the tax.
  • No lock-in: withdraw at any time.
  • Not a deposit, and not capital-guaranteed. Values can fall.

Sources: Department of Finance, Budget 2027: Tax Policy Changes, 6 October 2026, and the Roadmap for the Taxation of Retail Investment, 31 August 2026.

Still to come

Proposed · as at 8 October 2026

  • The law itself: the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026, due in October 2026.
  • Providers and their fees: not yet published. Investment firms, fund managers and insurers can offer it.
  • More than one account each: may be allowed later.

Until the law passes, any of this can change.

Tax today, outside the account

  • Funds and ETFs: 38% exit tax on the gain, falling to 35% from 1 January 2027 (Budget 2027), also charged on a deemed disposal every eight years. Source: Revenue, Tax and Duty Manual Part 27-01A-02, Investment Undertakings (updated January 2026).
  • Shares: 31% CGT on the gain for sales from 7 October 2026 (it was 33%), after an exemption of €1,270 a year. Source: Revenue, How to calculate CGT.
  • Deposits: 33% Deposit Interest Retention Tax (DIRT) on the interest. Source: Revenue, What DIRT rate is applicable?

Who it might suit

Proposed · as at 8 October 2026

Your employer pays into a pension

A pension gets employer money and tax relief. The PIA gets neither.

You run your own company

Your company can pay into your pension. A PIA is paid from your own money. See pensions for company directors.

You need the money before 60

A pension is normally locked until 60. The PIA is not.

How we worked this out
  • Not included: your old pensions, fees and charges, your employer’s scheme.
  • Only the pension adds tax relief: at your rate, within Revenue’s age-related limit each year.
  • All three grow at the same rate, monthly, with payments at each month end.
  • PIA tax: your rate on the average value above your tax-free amount, taken each year end. The real tax uses the average of daily values; this uses twelve month-end values. At most €12,000 a year goes in.
  • The ETF accumulates. Each month’s purchase has a deemed disposal every eighth anniversary: 35% (the rate from 1 January 2027) of the gain since the last, paid by selling units. At the end all is sold, with 35% exit tax on any gain left. A fall after a deemed disposal gets that tax back, up to the tax on the fall.
  • The pension is taken at the end, in one go: a quarter as a lump sum in Revenue’s bands, the rest as income at your rate. In practice the rest is usually drawn over years, perhaps at another rate.
