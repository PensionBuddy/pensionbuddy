The PIA in brief

  • 1% a yearon the average daily value above €50,000, due even in a falling year. The provider pays it.
  • €12,000 a yearthe most that can go in
  • 1 July 2027it opens. One each, for Irish tax residents aged 18 or over.

Holds listed shares, bonds, funds, ETFs and insurance-based investments. Not a deposit: values can fall. Still to come: the law itself, and the providers and their fees.

This page is information, not advice.

How we worked this out
  • The same take-home amount goes into each at every month end. All three grow at the same rate.
  • Pension: tax relief at your rate, assuming your payments are within Revenue’s age-related limit. Taken at the end in one go: a quarter as a lump sum in Revenue’s bands, the rest taxed as income at your rate. In practice most is drawn over years.
  • PIA: the rate on the average value above the tax-free amount, taken at each year end. The real tax uses daily values; this uses month-end values.
  • ETF: accumulating, with a deemed disposal of each month’s purchase every eight years and exit tax at the end, both at 35% (38% before 1 January 2027). Revenue, Tax and Duty Manual Part 27-01A-02.
  • Not included: fees and charges, inflation, employer money.
  • The PIA is proposed in Budget 2027. It becomes law only when the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 is passed. Department of Finance, Budget 2027: Tax Policy Changes, 6 October 2026, and the Roadmap for the Taxation of Retail Investment, 31 August 2026.
