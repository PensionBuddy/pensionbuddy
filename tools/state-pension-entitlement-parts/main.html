How the two methods work

Method 1, the Total Contributions Approach (TCA), adds up paid, credited and HomeCaring Periods. A full record, 2,080, gets the maximum personal rate: €299.30 a week from January 2026. Less is pro rata.

The Yearly Average method divides paid and credited contributions by the years from your first PRSI year to the year before you turn 66. Rounded to a whole number, that sets the band.

Method 2 mixes the two rates. The Yearly Average share falls ten points a year, fixed by the year your pension starts. From 2034 only the TCA applies.

The assumptions

  • The pension starts at 66, in 2025 or later.
  • Left out, though each could only raise a figure: the Homemaker's Scheme, the Alternative Yearly Average, voluntary contributions, Long-Term Carer's Contributions and contributions in the year you turn 66. So the figures are a floor.
  • Not covered: the means-tested State Pension (Non-Contributory), and mixed-rate, European Union or pro-rata records, which can give less or nothing.
  • You must first pay PRSI before 56. This page does not check that.
  • The personal rate at 66, without increases for a qualified adult or child, or at 80. A year is 52 weekly payments.
