How the two methods work

Method 1, the Total Contributions Approach (TCA), adds paid, credited and HomeCaring Periods. A full record, 2,080 or forty years, gets the maximum personal rate: €299.30 a week from January 2026, €309.30 from January 2027 (Budget 2027). Less is pro rata.

The Yearly Average method divides paid and credited contributions, at most 52 a year, by the years from your first PRSI year to the year before you turn 66. The result, rounded half up to a whole number, sets the band.

Method 2 adds a share of the Yearly Average rate to a share of the TCA rate. The Yearly Average share falls ten points a year. The start year fixes it for good. From 2034 only the TCA applies.

How we worked this out
  • Not included: your old pensions, your tax position, your employer’s scheme.
  • Below the qualifying minimum there is no entitlement under either method. Credited contributions and HomeCaring Periods never count toward it. The means-tested State Pension (Non-Contributory) is not calculated.
  • This page assumes the pension starts at 66. Starting at 67 to 70 changes the mix and rate, and is not modelled. Band rates at those ages are not published for 2026.
  • The Homemaker's Scheme and the Alternative Yearly Average are left out. Each can only raise Method 2, so Method 2 is a floor on the details entered. The first drops up to twenty whole caring years from 1994 on, with no credited contributions, from the years divided by. The second counts from 1979, used only when it reaches 48.
  • Voluntary contributions and Long-Term Carer's Contributions are left out. Both count toward the 520, so could only raise a figure or bring someone over the minimum.
  • Contributions in the year you turn 66 are left out. So the TCA figure is a floor, by a few weeks' contributions at most.
  • Mixed-rate, European Union (EU) and pro-rata records are not covered. They can give less, or nothing, and are outside the floor statement.
  • The pension needs a first PRSI payment before 56. This page does not check that, and would still show a figure.
  • Entering the calendar year of a first payment from 1 January to 5 April, up to 2001, without ticking the April box, divides by one year too few. The figure may then be too high.
  • This page rounds Method 2 half up to the cent. No published rule covers it. At most that moves a figure half a cent a week.
  • Rates and rules change, usually at each Budget. A year is 52 weekly payments.
  • The personal rate at 66 excludes increases for a qualified adult or child, and the higher rate from 80.
  • Pensions that started before 2025 are not covered.
  • Band rates: the Department of Social Protection's Rates of Payment 2026 (SW19), page 33. Rules: Citizens Information. Your own record: MyWelfare.ie.
