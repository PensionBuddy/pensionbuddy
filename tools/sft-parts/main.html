The rules
  • Not included: what your pensions are worth, your tax position, a Personal Fund Threshold you may hold.
  • Finance Act 2024, section 13, sets the threshold. It was €2 million from 2014 to 2025. From 2030 it tracks Central Statistics Office average weekly earnings and cannot fall.
  • Budget 2027 keeps the threshold’s path (€2.4 million in 2027). It also revises the factors that value a defined benefit pension against it, from 1 January 2027; the new factors are due in the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026, and this page does not use them yet.
  • Each benefit is tested against all taken since 7 December 2005. Here, all are taken in one year.
  • Chargeable excess tax: any excess is taxed at 40% when taken, with no reliefs (Revenue). Up to €60,000 of 20% lump sum tax can be set against it. This page takes off the 20% tax on the lump sum you set.
  • What is left of an excess is taxed again as income when drawn: up to 68.8% in all, and more with Pay-Related Social Insurance (PRSI) (Department of Finance, independent examination of the threshold, 2024).
  • Lump sum: the €500,000 has been fixed since 1 January 2025 (Revenue Pensions Manual, chapter 27). Above it, a lump sum is taxed as income.
  • Figures are illustrations, not a guarantee.
