First payslip to 66

What builds up, and when.

66 pension age Toward a full record

  1. Your first payslip

    Pay-Related Social Insurance (PRSI) starts: 52 contributions a year toward your State Pension.

  2. Ten years in

    520 paid contributions: the minimum for any State Pension (Contributory). Credits and HomeCaring Periods do not count.

  3. All along the way

    Pension contributions get tax relief, rising with age.

  4. Forty years in

    2,080 reckonable contributions: a full record, for the maximum rate.

  5. Sixty-six

    Pension age.

Full-time caring can count toward the State Pension as HomeCaring Periods, up to 1,040 weeks. That is care of a child under 12, or of an older child or adult who needs an increased level of care. They count under the Total Contributions Approach once you have 520 paid contributions. You claim them when you apply. The State Pension entitlement check counts them in.

Once enrolled through payroll: what happens when.

  1. Months 7 and 8You can opt out and get back what you paid. Employer and State payments stay invested until you can draw them at 66.
  2. From month 7You can pause for one to two years. After month 8 you can pause but not opt out.
  3. Two years after an opt-outYou are enrolled again if under 66 and still in a job without a pension through payroll.
  4. 2029Rates double: 3% each from you and your employer, 1% from the State. Opting out in months 7 and 8 after the rise refunds only the extra the rise added.
  5. 20324.5% each, 1.5% from the State, and another opt-out window.
  6. 2035 on6% each, 2% from the State, and one more opt-out window.

My Future Fund started on 1 January 2026. It covers employees aged 23 to 59 earning €20,000 or more a year, with no pension through payroll. The self-employed are not included. More than 835,000 people were in it on 14 September 2026 (Department of Social Protection). Sources: the Automatic Enrolment Retirement Savings System Act 2024, sections 54, 55, 61, 62 and 63; gov.ie; Citizens Information.
